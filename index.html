The four questions people actually ask

The part nobody explained the first time

Most cable-company and big-brand systems run on proprietary hardware. The panel, the sensors, the cameras — all built to talk to one company's service and nothing else. That is a business decision, not a technical requirement, and it is why a service ending also ends your equipment.

Open-platform systems work differently. The panel speaks a published protocol, the sensors are standard, and if you change monitoring companies the hardware stays on the wall. You can be unhappy with your provider without being unhappy with your house.

Whatever you do next, the question worth asking any salesperson is simple: if I leave you in three years, does this equipment still work? If the answer is no, you are renting a system you paid to install.

        Your options, including the ones that are not us

        1. Do nothing yet. Your system may still arm. If it is not monitored, it is a noisemaker — but there is no emergency in deciding this week versus next month.
        2. Self-monitor. Buy an open-platform kit, install it yourself, get alerts on your phone. Cheapest path. No one calls the fire department if you are asleep or on a plane.
        3. Monitored, self-installed. Same equipment, add a monitoring plan. Most of the protection at a fraction of a traditional contract.
        4. Monitored, professionally installed. A licensed tech places and tests everything, pulls the permit, and it is warrantied. Costs more and is worth it on larger homes, or if you want fire and life safety done properly.

        Any of those four is a reasonable answer depending on the house and the budget.
